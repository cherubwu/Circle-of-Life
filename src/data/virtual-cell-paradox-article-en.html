CIRCLE OF LIFE / SCIENCE, PHILOSOPHY & A LITTLE REBELLION / NOV 2026

The Virtual
Cell Paradox.

If the final test of a therapy happens in a living body, why are we so eager to recreate a cell in silicon?
Can we predict life without understanding it?

A hundred million cells in a computer sounds like a magnificent expedition. But what exactly have we brought home—a law of life, or a spectacularly detailed map that cannot tell us where to go next?

I love data. Vast cell atlases make age, obesity, disease and drug exposure look as though they belong on one map. Yet anyone who has spent time in the lab knows that cells are stubborn. Their behaviour depends on their history, neighbours, and many things we have not measured.

So the question is not whether AI will replace biologists. It is this: when does a model trained on yesterday’s experiments earn the right to guide tomorrow’s?

01
ORIGINS / THE IDEA IS OLDER THAN AI

The virtual cell was never one single idea.

The term did not suddenly appear in 2024. For decades, systems biologists have tried to write down biochemical reactions, diffusion and cellular dynamics. The ambition remains familiar—make a living system predictable—but the mathematical meaning of “simulation” has shifted dramatically.

1990s–2000s / Mechanistic virtual cells

VCell and related tools describe reaction kinetics, compartmental diffusion and membrane transport. Their assumptions are explicit, even when their parameters remain incomplete. [1]

2012 / Whole-cell simulation

Karr and colleagues integrated cellular processes in a model of Mycoplasma genitalium. A pioneering bacterial model, not a human-cell digital twin. [2]

2016–2021 / Perturb-seq

CRISPR coupled to single-cell RNA-seq changed the task from describing existing cell states to measuring how genetic interventions change them. The readout, however, is mainly RNA. [3]

2023–2024 / Foundation models

Geneformer, scGPT and scFoundation explored transferable cellular representations. The 2024 Cell perspective articulated the vision of an AI virtual cell. [4]

2025–2026 / The verification era

Models such as STATE, STACK and X-Cell pushed perturbation prediction forward as independent benchmarks exposed limitations of simple scaling, data quality and evaluation metrics. [5][6]

A / MECHANISTIC

A cell of equations

Explicit rates and mechanisms, but limited coverage and parameter identifiability.

B / REPRESENTATIONAL

A cell of vectors

Learn which cells resemble one another. A useful embedding is not necessarily a useful intervention model.

C / PREDICTIVE

A cell of transitions

Given a state, perturbation and context, predict what comes next—and test that prediction externally.

A cell is not a transcriptome.
A simulation is not an explanation.
A prediction is not yet a discovery.

Recent reviews increasingly urge us to specify a model’s actual context of use rather than score how closely it resembles an abstract idea of “life.” [8][9]

02
HORIZONTAL COMPARISON / AGE, OBESITY, PROGRESSION

Can we compare cells across age, obesity and disease progression?

Yes—and such comparisons may be among the most exciting achievements of modern cell atlases. But an atlas of 20-year-olds and 70-year-olds is not a time-lapse of the same cell growing old. We must distinguish changes in cell composition from changes within cell types.

Two points that sit together on a UMAP may be the same cell type, yet come from people separated by half a century. Closeness in embedding space does not mean time has passed between them.

The point is not to choose between composition and within-cell-type expression. Both can be biologically important. Analyse them separately and return to donors, clinical metadata and functional evidence.

Cross-sectional is not longitudinal

Cross-sectional

Different people, different ages

You can estimate group differences, but cohort effects and survival selection remain possible explanations.

Longitudinal

The same person over time

You can estimate within-person changes, but repeated-measures statistics, timing and attrition still matter.

Pseudotime is an inferred ordering in cellular state space. It is not calendar time and does not prove a natural disease trajectory. [10]

My rule: if age, disease or obesity status is inseparable from batch, no integration algorithm can discover from the expression matrix alone which difference was technical and which was biological.
03
VERTICAL COMPARISON / EVOLUTION IS NOT A BATCH

Mouse and human cells can share a model. But evolution is not a batch effect.

A conserved pathway across species is exciting precisely because it survived evolutionary change. Yet a human and a mouse are not two translations of the same book. Similar cell names do not guarantee equivalent physiology, disease mechanisms, drug exposures or developmental time.

Meaningful comparisons

One-to-one orthologs, conserved gene programs, comparable cell types, matched perturbations and consistent direction of pathway responses.

Dangerous equivalences

The same chronological age, identical drug dose, apparently matching UMAP clusters, or the assumption that mouse disease models recapitulate human clinical outcomes.

These are evidence checks, not a validated confidence score or regulatory status.A seven-species benchmark evaluated 13 integration methods in 2026; broader cross-species work also documents a trade-off between batch mixing and biological conservation.[11][12]

Beware overcorrection. A beautiful mixed-species UMAP can conceal genuine species-specific biology. Report biological conservation and batch mixing, not one picture.

The hardest test lies at the intersection: can a model trained on healthy young mouse cells predict a drug response in an unseen older human donor with obesity? That combines species, age, disease and intervention shifts. Success on each separately does not guarantee success on all at once. [11][12]

DATA QUALITY / THE EXPERIMENT BEHIND THE FILE

A billion cells cannot rescue a confused experiment.

More reads do not repair an unrecorded donor, a disease group perfectly confounded with sequencing batch, or a failed guide RNA. Data quality is not one universal mitochondrial-read cutoff: it is the combination of provenance, biological replicates, experimental design, transparent QC and independent validation.

05
THE BENCHMARK DEBATE / 2025 VS 2026

The models did not change overnight. Our exam changed.

The 2025 Nature Methods study asked whether seven deep-learning perturbation predictors beat simple mean, additive or linear baselines. Under its tested tasks and metrics, they generally did not. [6]

The 2026 Nature Biotechnology response asked a different question: if only a few genes truly respond, can ordinary MSE even reward a model for finding them? Positive and negative references were used to calibrate 18 metrics. Some deep models then outperformed baselines in defined settings. [7]

2025 / A serious warning

Complexity, pretraining scale and elegant embedding do not guarantee better unseen perturbation predictions than simple baselines.

2026 / A necessary refinement

Global error can be dominated by thousands of unchanged genes. A metric should be sensitive to genuine perturbation-specific effects.

What counts as a successful prediction?

Neither paper demonstrated a complete virtual cell. Both primarily evaluated transcriptomic predictions under specified experimental contexts. A model can succeed on a weighted RNA metric yet fail at protein signalling, cell death or cross-species transfer. Metric calibration is an advance in evaluation, not a licence to skip the lab.

07
THE GLOBAL LANDSCAPE / WHO OWNS WHICH LAYER?

Sixteen organisations. Not sixteen versions of the same cell.

Some groups build atlases, some generate perturbation data, others train predictive models, and a few must answer to actual drug pipelines. Comparing them by model parameter count or funding rounds alone is like comparing a microscope, a hospital and a sequencing instrument by weight.

Open each organisation to see its evidence, defensibility, limitations, next proof point and source. Company claims are not independent validation.

Five tests of durable value

01 · Distinctive data

Independent donors, reproducible perturbations, dose and time variation, and unsuccessful experiments—not just a large cell count.

02 · Generalisation

External study, donor, cell type and perturbation performance against strong baselines.

03 · Cross-modal reach

Protein, imaging and functional outputs—not only RNA reconstruction.

04 · Data–experiment loop

Transparent and reproducible experiments that target model uncertainty.

05 · Translation

Prospectively improved decisions, less unnecessary lab work and concrete programme milestones.

Public academic institutions such as Arc and Biohub are building shared infrastructure. Tahoe is best understood through perturbation-data production. Xaira and others focus on predictive architectures. Recursion and insitro connect models to drug projects. Chinese teams have contributed their own directions, from scFoundation to dynamic proteomics. These are different businesses with different proofs of success.

08
TRANSLATION / THE FINAL MILE IS REAL

If the patient is real, why simulate the cell?

Because experimental space is vast, samples are precious, and no laboratory can test every combination. But a virtual cell is valuable only if it helps us choose better experiments, not if it merely creates another expensive validation step.

FROM MY NOTEBOOK ✦The last mile of science is still in the real world.

If a computational forecast makes the next experiment more informative, it is useful. If it costs months of work to validate a speculative ranking that never improves discovery, it is a burden.

Could a virtual cell replace clinical trials?

Most credible now

Earlier prioritisation

Within a restricted cell, perturbation class and validated endpoint, improve candidate ranking and experiment selection.

Context-dependent

Nonclinical evidence

Complement organoids, organs-on-chips, animal and toxicology studies with rigorously validated models.

Not established

General replacement of human trials

Cellular RNA does not resolve systemic PK/PD, long-term toxicity, clinical heterogeneity or patient outcomes.

The FDA’s 2026 NAMs draft addresses validation under defined contexts of use; it is not approval to replace all living-system evidence with an algorithm. [20]

The promise of a virtual cell is not that we stop experimenting.
It is that we learn to ask better experiments.

One question to leave open

If a model predicts RNA responses to an unseen TP53 or KRAS perturbation but fails on protein activity, cell death or cross-species transfer, has it succeeded?

I am not against virtual cells. I hope they will one day tell us which experiment could overturn a cherished hypothesis—and when they do not know. A virtual cell need not breathe or die. It must help us understand living cells with less certainty and more evidence.
